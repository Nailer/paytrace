## Blockchain

A **blockchain** is an append-only, replicated ledger in which transactions are batched into blocks; each block commits to its predecessor via a cryptographic hash, and network validators reach consensus on a single canonical chain.

<Accordion title="In plain English" icon="lightbulb">
Think of a giant public notebook copied onto thousands of computers. Every few moments, a new page (a **block**) of transactions is added. Each page references the previous one, so nobody can secretly rewrite history. Everyone can read it; nobody can erase it.
</Accordion>

## Monad and Monad Testnet

**Monad** is a high-throughput, EVM-compatible Layer-1 blockchain. **Monad Testnet** is its public test network, identified by **chain ID `10143`** (hex `0x279f`), with native gas token **MON**. PayTrace uses the public RPC `https://testnet-rpc.monad.xyz` and the explorer `https://testnet.monadscan.com`.

<Accordion title="In plain English" icon="lightbulb">
Monad is one particular blockchain. It works like Ethereum, so Ethereum wallets like MetaMask can use it. The **testnet** is a practice version where coins are free and worth nothing, perfect for trying things without risking real money. The **chain ID** (10143) is just its unique number so your wallet knows which network it's on.
</Accordion>

## Wallet and address

A **wallet** manages a private key and derives a 20-byte **address** (`0x` + 40 hex characters) from its public key. An **externally owned account (EOA)** is an address controlled directly by such a key, as opposed to a smart-contract account. Browser wallets expose an **EIP-1193** provider (`window.ethereum`) for websites to request accounts, signatures and transactions.

<Accordion title="In plain English" icon="lightbulb">
A wallet is an app (like MetaMask, Rabby or Coinbase Wallet) that holds your secret key. Your **address** is like your account number: `0x` followed by 40 letters and numbers. You can share it freely. Your secret key (or "seed phrase") you never share, with anyone, ever, including PayTrace. When a website wants you to pay or sign something, your wallet pops up and asks for your approval.
</Accordion>

## USDC and ERC-20 tokens

**USDC** is a dollar-referenced stablecoin issued by Circle. On EVM chains it is an **ERC-20** token contract. Balances live in the contract's storage; a transfer is a call to `transfer(address to, uint256 amount)` (function selector `0xa9059cbb`), which emits a `Transfer(address indexed from, address indexed to, uint256 value)` event. USDC uses **6 decimals**, so `1 USDC = 1,000,000` base units. PayTrace only accepts Circle's test USDC at `0x534b2f3A21130d7a60830c2Df862319e593943A3`.

<Accordion title="In plain English" icon="lightbulb">
USDC is a digital dollar. On the blockchain, it's managed by a program (a "token contract") that keeps track of who owns how much. Sending USDC means asking that program to move some from you to someone else, and the program writes a public note saying "A sent B this much." PayTrace reads that note.

**Important:** anyone can create a fake coin and name it "USDC." That's why PayTrace checks the exact contract address, not the name.
</Accordion>

## Gas and MON

Every transaction consumes **gas**, paid in the chain's native asset. On Monad Testnet that is **MON**. A USDC transfer therefore requires the payer to hold both test USDC (the value) and a small amount of test MON (the fee).

<Accordion title="In plain English" icon="lightbulb">
Sending anything on a blockchain costs a tiny fee, like a stamp on an envelope. On Monad Testnet the fee is paid in **MON**. So to pay 10 test USDC, you need the 10 USDC *plus* a little MON for the fee. Both are free on the testnet from faucets. See [Get test tokens](/guides/get-test-tokens).
</Accordion>

## Transaction hash, receipt and logs

A **transaction hash** is the 32-byte Keccak-256 identifier (`0x` + 64 hex). After inclusion, a **transaction receipt** reports `status` (`0x1` success, `0x0` reverted), `blockNumber`, `blockHash` and an array of **logs** (events). Each log has an `address` (emitting contract), `topics` (event signature and indexed params), `data` and a block-unique `logIndex`.

<Accordion title="In plain English" icon="lightbulb">
- The **transaction hash** is the tracking number of a payment. Your wallet shows it after you send.
- The **receipt** is the official result: did it succeed or fail, and which page (block) is it on?
- The **logs** are the notes written during the transaction, like "USDC: Ada sent you 25." PayTrace reads these.
</Accordion>

## Finality

A block is **final** when the consensus protocol guarantees it cannot be reverted. JSON-RPC exposes the latest final block via the `finalized` block tag. A block is **canonical** if the block at its height on the provider's current chain has the same hash.

<Accordion title="In plain English" icon="lightbulb">
Right after a payment, it's "written in pencil." A moment later it becomes "written in ink," permanent. PayTrace waits for the ink. It also double-checks that the page the payment is on is the real, official page, not a draft that got thrown away. More in [Finality](/concepts/finality).
</Accordion>

## Faucet

A **faucet** is a service that dispenses free testnet tokens. Circle's faucet ([faucet.circle.com](https://faucet.circle.com)) provides test USDC on Monad Testnet.

<Accordion title="In plain English" icon="lightbulb">
A faucet is a free tap for play money. You paste your address and it sends you test coins so you can try PayTrace.
</Accordion>

## Signing a message vs sending a transaction

**Signing** (`personal_sign`) produces an ECDSA signature over an arbitrary message, proving control of an address without changing chain state or costing gas. **Sending a transaction** (`eth_sendTransaction`) broadcasts a state change that consumes gas and may move value.

<Accordion title="In plain English" icon="lightbulb">
- **Signing a message** = showing your ID. Free, moves no money. PayTrace uses this to log you in.
- **Sending a transaction** = actually paying. Costs a small fee, moves money. PayTrace only asks for this on the checkout page, and your wallet always shows you exactly what you're approving.

**Golden rule:** read every wallet popup. If a site asks you to "approve" unlimited spending of your tokens, that's a different (riskier) thing. PayTrace never asks for that.
</Accordion>
